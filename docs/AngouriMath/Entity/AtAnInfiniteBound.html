AtAnInfiniteBound​(AngouriMath.​Entity,​AngouriMath.​Entity.​Variable,​AngouriMath.​Entity,​AngouriMath.​Entity)

 Method (no overloads)

Summary

The antiderivative's limit at an infinite bound, where substituting the bound left an infinity inside
the value, or null to keep the substituted value as it was.

Remarks

erf(+oo - y) is left as it is by the arithmetic, since y could itself be infinite; but an
integral with a parameter is a function of that parameter at each of its finite values, and the limit
of erf(t - y) as t grows reads y as exactly that, a finite constant, and is 1. So
e^(-(t - y)^2) over the line is sqrt(pi) rather than a difference of two unsettled error
functions. https://github.com/asc-community/AngouriMath/issues/2451
 It is taken only where the antiderivative was read to be continuous across the range, so that the limit
at the end is the whole integral and not the last piece of it: 1/(t^2 + y^2)'s antiderivative
for y^2 < 0 is a logarithm with zeros at t = ±sqrt(-y^2), which cannot be listed for a
symbolic y, and taking its limits would answer 0, the principal value, where a concrete y = i answers NaN. What the breaks read is the real line with the parameters real: arctan(t - y) is continuous in t for every real y, but for |Im y| > 1 it crosses arctan's cut,
and the integral of 1/(1 + (t - y)^2) over the line is 0 there and not pi. A symbol is read as
real wherever the breaks are read, so that is the answer for a symbolic y; at a concrete y off
the line the crossing is placed, and the answer is 0 (see AcrossTheCuts(AngouriMath.Entity,AngouriMath.Entity.Variable,System.Collections.Generic.List{System.ValueTuple{AngouriMath.Entity,AngouriMath.Entity,AngouriMath.Entity.Number.Real,AngouriMath.Entity}},AngouriMath.Entity.Number.Real,AngouriMath.Entity.Number.Real,System.Collections.Generic.List{System.ValueTuple{AngouriMath.Entity,AngouriMath.Entity.Number.Real}})).
https://github.com/asc-community/AngouriMath/issues/2460
 A limit that is undecided, undefined, or still holds an infinity inside it -- e^(-y t) at
+oo depends on the sign of y, and the limit says so by staying e^(+oo * -y) -- leaves
the substituted value as it was.
